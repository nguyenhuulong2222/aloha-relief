Every dollar in. Every grant out. Out in the open.

After the 2023 Maui wildfires, help moved through personal payment links, crowdfunding pages and group chats. Donors couldn't see where money went, scams spread, and the same family could be paid twice while another waited. Aloha Relief is a small, supervised way for a community fund to do this better after the next disaster.

How a request becomes a grant

  1. Someone asks for help. Need, amount, island, a short description and a PayPal email.
    Requester
  2. Rules check it. Caps, duplicate payees, look-alike text, request bursts and hidden instructions are caught by plain, tested code.
    Deterministic rules
  3. AI explains it. Claude rates urgency, summarizes the need in plain words and explains every flag. It can't clear a rule or move money.
    Claude Haiku
  4. A volunteer decides. Approve, ask for more information or decline, always with a written reason.
    Human reviewer
  5. PayPal pays it. Approval sends a PayPal Payout. PayPal confirms delivery and the ledger updates.
    PayPal Payouts + webhooks

Rules check. AI explains. People decide. Aloha Relief is an open-source hackathon demo (MIT). The Kanaloa Relief Fund and everyone in it are fictional.
